## 1. DEFINITIONS

- **"Cloud LLM Provider"** means a third-party Large Language Model service that the Controller or its authorised users may activate from within the Services (such as Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, AWS Bedrock, Mistral AI, and others, as listed at https://dialekt.ai/legal/cloud-providers).

## 2. SCOPE AND ROLES

2.1 **Subject matter.** This DPA governs the Processor's processing of Personal Data on behalf of the Controller in connection with the Services.

2.2 **Roles of the Parties.** With respect to Personal Data described in Annex 1:

(a) the Controller is the controller; and
(b) the Processor is a processor, processing Personal Data only on the documented instructions of the Controller.

2.3 **Personal Data not within scope.** The Parties acknowledge that:

(a) **Local processing on user devices:** Personal Data processed locally on the Controller's authorised users' devices by the Services (including prompts, responses, chat history, and files) is not processed by the Processor on its infrastructure and is not within the scope of this DPA. With respect to such data, the Controller (or its authorised user) acts as the controller and operates the Services as the means of processing on its own behalf. The Processor has no technical means of access to such data.

(b) **Cloud LLM Provider routing:** Where the Controller or its authorised users activate a Cloud LLM Provider, prompts and responses are transmitted directly from the user's device to that provider's API, do not transit through the Processor's infrastructure, and are processed by the Cloud LLM Provider as an independent controller or processor under that provider's own terms. The Processor is not a processor of such data, and this DPA does not regulate that processing.

(c) **Account, billing, and authentication data:** With respect to data such as the names, email addresses, billing details, and authentication credentials of the Controller's authorised users (collectively, "**Administrative Data**"), the Processor acts as a controller as described in the Privacy Policy at https://dialekt.ai/legal/privacy. This DPA does not modify the Processor's obligations as a controller of Administrative Data.

2.4 **Documented instructions.** The Controller's instructions for processing Personal Data are set out in this DPA, the Principal Agreement, the Privacy Policy, and any further written instructions given by the Controller from time to time. The Processor will process Personal Data only on documented instructions from the Controller, including with regard to international transfers, unless required to do otherwise by law to which the Processor is subject; in such case the Processor will inform the Controller of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.

2.5 **Compliance with instructions.** If the Processor considers that an instruction from the Controller infringes Applicable Data Protection Law, the Processor will inform the Controller without delay.

## 3. PROCESSOR OBLIGATIONS

3.1 **Compliance.** The Processor will comply with all obligations of a processor under Applicable Data Protection Law.

3.2 **Confidentiality.** The Processor will ensure that any person authorised to process Personal Data under this DPA is bound by a written obligation of confidentiality or is under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality.

3.3 **Security.** The Processor will implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect Personal Data against unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction, damage, alteration, or disclosure. The measures currently in place are described in Annex 2 (Technical and Organisational Measures, "**TOM**").

3.4 **Sub-processors.** The Processor's engagement of Sub-processors is governed by Section 5.

3.5 **Assistance to the Controller.** Taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to the Processor, the Processor will assist the Controller, by appropriate technical and organisational measures, insofar as possible, for the fulfilment of the Controller's obligation to:

(a) respond to requests from Data Subjects exercising their rights under Articles 15–22 GDPR or equivalent provisions of other Applicable Data Protection Law;
(b) ensure compliance with the Controller's obligations under Articles 32–36 GDPR (security, breach notification, data protection impact assessments, and prior consultation), or equivalent obligations under other Applicable Data Protection Law;
(c) demonstrate compliance with Applicable Data Protection Law.

The Processor's obligations under this Section 3.5 are limited to the Personal Data that is within the scope of this DPA (Section 2). The Processor is not in a position to assist with Personal Data that is processed locally on user devices or routed directly to Cloud LLM Providers, as it has no technical means of access to such data.

3.6 **Records of processing.** The Processor will maintain a record of all categories of processing activities carried out on behalf of the Controller, in accordance with Article 30(2) GDPR.

3.7 **Audits.** The Processor will make available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with its obligations under this DPA, and will allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Controller or another auditor mandated by the Controller, subject to the conditions in Section 8.

3.8 **Personal Data Breach notification.** The Processor will notify the Controller without undue delay, and in any event within **48 hours**, after becoming aware of a Personal Data Breach affecting Personal Data processed under this DPA. The notification will include the information specified in Article 33(3) GDPR insofar as available, including:

(a) the nature of the Personal Data Breach including, where possible, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and Personal Data records concerned;
(b) the likely consequences of the Personal Data Breach;
(c) the measures taken or proposed to be taken to address the Personal Data Breach, including measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects.

Where it is not possible to provide all information at the same time, the information may be provided in phases without further undue delay. Notification of a Personal Data Breach does not constitute an admission by the Processor of fault or liability.

## 4. CONTROLLER OBLIGATIONS

4.1 **Lawful basis.** The Controller represents and warrants that it has all necessary rights, consents, and lawful bases under Applicable Data Protection Law for the processing of Personal Data instructed under this DPA, including for any disclosure to the Processor and Sub-processors.

4.2 **Notices and consents to data subjects.** The Controller is responsible for providing required notices to and obtaining required consents from Data Subjects in connection with the processing of Personal Data under this DPA.

4.3 **Authorised users.** The Controller is responsible for ensuring that its authorised users use the Services in accordance with the Principal Agreement, this DPA, and Applicable Data Protection Law, including with respect to any Cloud LLM Provider activation.

4.4 **Use of Cloud LLM Providers.** The Controller acknowledges that activation of any Cloud LLM Provider is at the Controller's (or its authorised users') sole discretion and risk, and that the Controller is solely responsible for:

(a) accepting the terms and privacy policy of each Cloud LLM Provider;
(b) ensuring a lawful basis for the disclosure of Personal Data to each Cloud LLM Provider;
(c) implementing any necessary safeguards for international transfers of Personal Data to Cloud LLM Providers;
(d) where the Controller operates in a regulated industry, activating `regulated_mode` to disable Cloud LLM Providers if required by sector-specific regulation.

4.5 **Sensitive Personal Data.** The Controller will not transmit to the Processor or to Cloud LLM Providers special categories of personal data within the meaning of Article 9 GDPR (or equivalent under other Applicable Data Protection Law), criminal conviction data, or data concerning children, unless the Controller has obtained explicit consent or has another lawful basis for the processing and has notified the Processor in writing in advance. Notification under this Section 4.5 is for awareness only; it does not by itself authorise the Processor to apply enhanced safeguards beyond those described in Annex 2.

## 7. RETURN OR DELETION OF PERSONAL DATA

7.1 **Upon termination.** Upon termination of the Principal Agreement, or at any earlier time on written request of the Controller, the Processor will, at the Controller's choice:

(a) delete all Personal Data processed on behalf of the Controller; or
(b) return all such Personal Data to the Controller and delete existing copies,

unless storage of such Personal Data is required by Applicable Data Protection Law or other applicable law.

7.2 **Default.** In the absence of an instruction from the Controller within 30 days of the termination of the Principal Agreement, the Processor will delete the Personal Data in accordance with the retention schedule in the Privacy Policy.

7.3 **Backups.** Personal Data residing in encrypted backup snapshots that have been excluded from active processing will be deleted in the ordinary course of backup rotation, within a maximum of 7 days following deletion from active systems.

7.4 **Certification.** Upon written request, the Processor will certify in writing the deletion of Personal Data under this Section 7.

7.5 **Locally stored data.** This Section 7 does not apply to Personal Data stored locally on the Controller's authorised users' devices. Such data is under the Controller's exclusive control and the Controller is responsible for its retention and deletion.

---

## 8. AUDITS

8.1 **Audit rights.** Subject to the conditions in this Section 8, the Controller may audit the Processor's compliance with this DPA up to **once per calendar year**.

8.2 **Conditions.** Any audit under this DPA is subject to the following:

(a) the Controller will give the Processor at least **30 days' prior written notice** of any audit, except in cases following a Personal Data Breach affecting the Controller, in which case the notice period may be shorter;
(b) audits will be conducted during regular business hours and in a manner that does not unreasonably disrupt the Processor's operations;
(c) the Controller's auditor must execute a confidentiality undertaking acceptable to the Processor;
(d) the Controller's auditor must not be a competitor of the Processor;
(e) the audit must be limited in scope to matters specific to the Controller and to the Personal Data processed under this DPA, and must not extend to other customers' data or generic infrastructure assessments;
(f) the costs of the audit are borne by the Controller, except where the audit reveals a material breach of this DPA, in which case the Processor will reimburse the Controller's reasonable costs.

8.3 **Audit reports as alternative.** The Processor may satisfy its audit obligations by providing the Controller with summary reports of independent third-party audits or certifications (such as SOC 2, ISO 27001, or equivalent), where such reports cover the Processor's compliance with the obligations relevant to this DPA. Such reports are Confidential Information of the Processor.

## 9. LIABILITY

9.1 **Liability cap.** The aggregate liability of each Party arising out of or in connection with this DPA is subject to the limitation of liability set out in the Principal Agreement.

9.2 **Apportionment of regulatory fines.** Where a regulatory fine is imposed jointly on the Parties under Applicable Data Protection Law, each Party will bear the fine in proportion to its responsibility for the infringement, as determined by the Supervisory Authority or by a court of competent jurisdiction.

9.3 **Carve-outs.** Nothing in this Section 9 limits liability that cannot be limited under Applicable Data Protection Law or other mandatory law.

## 10. TERM AND TERMINATION

10.1 **Term.** This DPA enters into force on the date it is signed by both Parties (or, where this DPA is incorporated into the Principal Agreement, on the effective date of the Principal Agreement) and remains in force for the duration of the Processor's processing of Personal Data on behalf of the Controller.

10.2 **Termination.** This DPA terminates automatically upon the termination of the Principal Agreement, subject to the survival of obligations that by their nature should survive (including Sections 7, 9, 10.2, and 11).

10.3 **Effect of termination.** Upon termination of this DPA, the Processor will return or delete Personal Data in accordance with Section 7.

## 11. GENERAL

11.1 **Order of precedence.** In the event of a conflict between this DPA and the Principal Agreement, this DPA prevails to the extent of the conflict, with respect to the processing of Personal Data. In the event of a conflict between this DPA and the SCCs (where the SCCs apply pursuant to Section 6.1), the SCCs prevail.

11.2 **Severability.** If any provision of this DPA is held invalid or unenforceable, that provision will be modified to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable, or, if not possible, severed. The remaining provisions remain in full force.

11.3 **Variation.** This DPA may be varied only by written instrument signed by both Parties, except that the Processor may update Annex 2 (TOM) and Annex 3 (Sub-processors) from time to time in accordance with Section 5.4 of this DPA without requiring re-execution.

11.4 **Notices.** Notices under this DPA must be sent in writing:

(a) to the Processor: legal@dias.now and by post to the registered office set out at the start of this DPA;
(b) to the Controller: at the email and postal address set out in Annex 1 or as otherwise notified by the Controller from time to time.

11.5 **Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this DPA without the prior written consent of the other Party, except that either Party may assign this DPA to an Affiliate or in connection with a merger, acquisition, or sale of substantially all assets, on notice to the other Party.

11.6 **Counterparts.** This DPA may be executed in counterparts, including by electronic signature, each of which is deemed an original and all of which together constitute one and the same instrument.

11.7 **Governing law.** This DPA is governed by the law specified in the Principal Agreement, except that with respect to obligations arising under the SCCs, the governing law is as set out in Section 6.1.

# ANNEX 2 — TECHNICAL AND ORGANISATIONAL MEASURES (TOM)

The Processor implements and maintains the following technical and organisational measures to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, in accordance with Article 32 GDPR.

## 1. Pseudonymisation and encryption of Personal Data

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Encryption in transit | All communications with the Services are protected by TLS 1.2 or higher (HTTPS). Email transmission uses STARTTLS or implicit TLS on standard ports. |
| Encryption at rest | The production PostgreSQL database is hosted on encrypted file systems. API keys for Cloud LLM Providers are stored in the operating system's secure keychain on the user's local device. |
| Key management | Encryption keys are managed in accordance with industry best practice. Production keys are not accessible to development personnel. |

## 2. Confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of processing systems

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Access control | Role-based access control (RBAC) is enforced for all administrative endpoints. Authentication is required for all cloud-side operations. |
| Multi-factor authentication | Mandatory for all administrative accounts with access to customer Personal Data. |
| Audit logging | All administrative actions on customer tenants are recorded in an immutable audit log retained for 3 years. |
| Network security | Production infrastructure is protected by a Web Application Firewall (Cloudflare). DDoS protection is enabled. |
| Vulnerability management | Production systems are patched in line with vendor recommendations. Critical security patches are applied within 7 days of release. |
| Endpoint security | Personnel devices accessing production systems are subject to baseline security requirements (full-disk encryption, screen lock, OS updates). |

## 3. Restoration of availability and access following an incident

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Backups | Daily encrypted snapshots of the production PostgreSQL database. |
| Backup retention | 7-day rotation cycle. |
| Restoration testing | Backup restoration is tested at least quarterly. |
| Disaster recovery | A documented disaster recovery procedure is maintained and reviewed at least annually. |

## 4. Regular testing, assessment, and evaluation

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Process review | Information security procedures are reviewed at least annually. |
| Code review | All changes to production code undergo peer review prior to deployment. |
| Penetration testing | External penetration testing is performed prior to general availability of new major releases of the Services and at least annually thereafter, where the Processor's stage of development warrants. |
| Logging and monitoring | Security-relevant events are logged. Anomalies are reviewed manually. |

## 5. User identification and authorisation

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Unique credentials | Each authorised user is identified by a unique account. Shared accounts are not permitted. |
| Password policy | Strong password requirements are enforced; passwords are stored hashed using a modern algorithm (Argon2 or bcrypt). |

## 6. Data minimisation, accuracy, and storage limitation

| Measure | Description |
|---|---|
| Data minimisation | Only the data necessary for the Services is collected, in accordance with the Privacy Policy. |
| Accuracy | Authorised users may correct their account data via the user interface or by contacting privacy@dias.now. |

## 7. Personnel measures

## 9. Incident management

# ANNEX 4 — JURISDICTION-SPECIFIC ADDENDA

## A. Kazakhstan-specific provisions

Where the Controller is established in the Republic of Kazakhstan or processes Personal Data subject to the Kazakhstan Data Protection Law:

A.1 The Processor is registered as a personal data operator with the Personal Data Protection Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan; the Controller's instruction to process Personal Data does not transfer the operator status to the Processor.

A.2 Where the Controller transfers Personal Data subject to the Kazakhstan Data Protection Law to the Processor or its Sub-processors located outside Kazakhstan, the Controller represents and warrants that it has obtained the consents required under Articles 7–8 of the Kazakhstan Data Protection Law from the relevant Data Subjects, or that another lawful basis for such transfer applies.

A.3 The Processor will assist the Controller, upon reasonable request, in responding to inquiries or actions of the Personal Data Protection Committee of the Republic of Kazakhstan affecting Personal Data processed under this DPA.

## B. UK-specific provisions

Where Personal Data subject to the UK GDPR is processed under this DPA, the UK Addendum referenced in Section 6.2 of the DPA applies.

## C. Swiss-specific provisions

Where Personal Data subject to the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection is processed under this DPA, the modifications set out in Section 6.3 of the DPA apply.
